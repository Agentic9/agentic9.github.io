AI teammates you message like colleagues.

Agentic9 Crew gives you persistent, named bots. Each has its own computer, its own memory and its own connected tools. They run tasks end to end while your devices are off, hand work to each other, and come back only when something needs your approval.

How it works

Give a bot a name, a title and a description of what it does. Then talk to it. It plans, works in its own sandbox, delegates to teammates when the job crosses into their remit, and pauses only for the decisions you want to make yourself.

you

Ops, pull last month's invoices from Xero, reconcile against the bank feed and draft the summary for Friday.

Ops

On it. Reconciling now. Two invoices do not match; asking Finance to check the FX rates before I write anything up.

Finance

Rates confirmed. Difference is a bank fee, not an error. Back to you, Ops.

Ops

Summary drafted. I need approval to send it to the board list. One tap in the app.

Six traits

Three of these are what set Agentic9 apart: mobile first, multi-agent, and real data connections. The other three make the whole thing dependable.

01

A computer per agent

Every bot has its own cloud sandbox with a filesystem, a terminal and a browser. Work persists between conversations; nothing runs on your laptop.

02

Multi-bot collaboration

Bots have a name, a title and a description. Delegation is routed on those descriptions, and group threads let several bots and people work the same problem.

03

Mobile first and always on

Designed for the phone in your pocket. Bots keep working while your devices are off and reach you with a push notification when it matters.

04

Data connections

OAuth connectors and custom MCP servers, scoped per bot. Your sales bot sees the CRM; your ops bot does not, unless you say so.

05

Routines and triggers

A 07:00 daily brief. Act on every new email. Watch a Slack channel. Schedules and events start work without you starting it.

06

Selective human approval

Autonomous by default. Sensitive actions pause and wait for one-tap approval via push notification, then carry on exactly where they left off.

Built in the open, on Cloudflare

Agentic9 is written in TypeScript with a hexagonal architecture, so the model provider is a port rather than a dependency. Claude comes first; open-source models via Workers AI follow. The code is open source on GitHub.
